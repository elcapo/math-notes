cf_algebra_01_linear_from_points	You have two points of a linear function: (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂). Without plugging into a formula, derive the slope a and intercept b. Why do you need two points?	Step 1 — find a: the slope is the <b>vertical change per unit of horizontal change</b> between the points: a = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁).<br>Step 2 — find b: substitute one point into y = ax + b and solve for b.<br>You need two points because a linear function has <b>two free parameters</b> (a and b); each point gives one equation. One point determines b given a, but a itself requires comparing two positions.	algebra linear-functions	Basic	Maths::Calculus Foundations::Algebra

Equation: f(x) = a/(x − h) + k.<br>Substitute the point: y₀ = a/(x₀ − h) + k ⇒ a = (y₀ − k)(x₀ − h).<br><b>Uniqueness:</b> yes — three constraints (h from vertical asymptote, k from horizontal asymptote, the point) pin the three parameters (h, k, a). cf_algebra_03_sqrt_a2_abs	Why is √(a²) = |a| and not simply a? Give a numeric example that shows the difference.	The square root notation always returns the <b>non-negative</b> value. Since a² ≥ 0 for any real a, √(a²) must be ≥ 0.<br><b>Example:</b> a = −3. Then a² = 9, √9 = 3, but a = −3. So √(a²) = 3 = |−3|, not −3.<br><b>Principle:</b> the function √x is defined as the non-negative root; the "±" appears only when <i>solving</i> x² = 9, not when <i>taking</i> the square root.	algebra radicals	Basic	Maths::Calculus Foundations::Algebra

cf_algebra_05_difference_of_squares	Factor the expression 4x² − 9. Then solve 4x² − 9 = 7. What algebraic principle connects both steps?	<b>Factoring:</b> 4x² − 9 = (2x)² − 3² = (2x − 3)(2x + 3).<br><b>Solving:</b> 4x² − 9 = 7 ⇒ 4x² = 16 ⇒ x² = 4 ⇒ x = ±2.<br><b>Principle:</b> the difference of squares identity a² − b² = (a−b)(a+b) reverses the expansion (ax + b)(ax − b) = a²x² − b². cf_algebra_06_derive_sec2	Starting from sin²x + cos²x = 1, derive the identity tan²x + 1 = sec²x. What did you divide by, and why does it require cos x ≠ 0?	Divide both sides by cos²x:<br>(sin²x/cos²x) + (cos²x/cos²x) = 1/cos²x<br>⇒ tan²x + 1 = sec²x.<br><b>Why divide by cos²x?</b> Because tan = sin/cos and sec = 1/cos — dividing by cos²x converts both to the target forms.<br><b>Domain note:</b> cos x ≠ 0 (x ≠ π/2 + kπ) because sec is undefined there. The identity holds on the domain where both sides are defined.	algebra trigonometry-identity	Basic	Maths::Calculus Foundations::Algebra

cf_trig_02_trig_is_circular	Why is it more accurate to say trigonometry is about circular motion rather than triangles? Give one consequence of this perspective.	Triangles appear only as a <b>tool</b> to describe the position of a point on the circle using horizontal and vertical components. The primary object is the <b>unit circle</b> — not the triangle.<br><b>Consequence:</b> the periodic nature of sin and cos (they repeat every 2π) is obvious from circular motion, not from triangle geometry. The triangle picture obscures this fundamental periodicity.	trigonometry concept	Basic	Maths::Calculus Foundations::Trigonometry
